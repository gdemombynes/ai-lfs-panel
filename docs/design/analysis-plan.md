# Analysis plan (first pass)

Question: after generative AI became widely available (ChatGPT, November
2022), did employment in AI-exposed occupations grow more slowly than in less
exposed occupations in low- and middle-income countries, and was any gap
concentrated among young, entry-level workers?

Design follows Brynjolfsson, Chandar and Chen (2025), "Canaries in the Coal
Mine", adapted to survey microdata rather than payroll records, and is judged
against the null result in Humlum and Vestergaard (2025) for Denmark.

## Units and outcomes

Cells: country × calendar quarter × occupation (ISCO-08 at the deepest digit
level the country supports, capped at 3) × age group (15-21, 22-25, 26-29,
30-49, 50+) × sex × formality (`socialsec`). Cells with fewer than 30
unweighted observations are flagged and dropped in the main specification.

Outcomes per cell:

- log weighted employment;
- employment index, 2022 Q4 = 100;
- new-hire share: weighted mean of `tenure_lt12` (in the current job under 12
  months), the survey analogue of the hiring margin;
- young share within the occupation.

## Exposure

Primary: ILO 2025 GenAI occupational exposure (Gmyrek et al., ILO WP 140),
ISCO-08 4-digit scores and four gradients, aggregated to 3/2/1 digits both
unweighted and employment-weighted (pooled 2022 employment from the countries
with 4-digit codes). High exposure = top employment-weighted tercile at the
2022 baseline.

Robustness: Anthropic Economic Index observed usage by occupation, Felten
AIOE and Eloundou et al. GPT exposure, brought to ISCO-08 through the BLS
SOC-ISCO crosswalk; country-quarter adoption indices from `ai-usage-atlas`
to allow staggered treatment timing.

## Specification

Event study with 2022 Q4 as the reference quarter:

    y_{c,o,a,t} = Σ_k β_k · 1[t = k] · HighExp_o + FE_{c,o,a} + FE_{c,a,t} + ε

- `FE_{c,a,t}` absorbs country-specific seasonality, the post-COVID recovery
  and macro shocks by age group.
- Triple difference adds `HighExp × Young × Post`.
- Continuous-exposure variant replaces the tercile dummy.
- Survey-break fixed effects from the breaks table (Brazil 2025 Q3
  reweighting, Colombia 2022 Q1 redesign, Mexico ENOE-N, India PLFS 2025).
- Weights: cell employment. Standard errors clustered by country × isco3;
  wild bootstrap when interacting with country-level adoption (8 clusters).

Robustness: 2-digit cells; urban only; formal only; exposure measure swaps;
leave-one-country-out; pre-trends on the GLD 2018-2021 backfill.

## Caveats to state up front

- Sampling noise at 3 digits in a single quarter; expect to pool quarters.
- Informality and self-employment dilute any wage-employment effect.
- Argentina covers 31 urban areas only; India is urban-only before 2025.
- Adoption timing differs by country; the single Nov-2022 date is a
  simplification tested with the adoption indices.
- Only four pre-treatment quarters inside the 2022+ window; the GLD backfill
  supplies 2018-2021.
